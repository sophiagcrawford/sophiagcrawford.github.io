Hi, I'm Sophia Crawford.

I study why people sometimes choose to avoid compassion, and what caring costs us.

My research examines how practices like mindfulness shape the way we weigh the effort and rewards of caring for others, and what that means for our own well-being over time.

I am a first-year Ph.D. student in Social Psychology at The Pennsylvania State University, working with Dr. Daryl Cameron in the Empathy and Moral Psychology Lab. I received my B.S. in Psychology, summa cum laude with High Honors, from Radford University, where I was a research assistant in the Social and Affective Psychophysiology Lab under the mentorship of Dr. Daniel Berry and completed my honors thesis on how brief mindfulness practices influence compassion engagement and avoidance.

Research

Compassion is often treated as an automatic, unlimited response, but it takes effort, and people frequently choose to avoid it. My research examines the motivational costs of caring: when and why people approach or avoid compassion.

Mindfulness & Compassion Avoidance

Does mindfulness heighten awareness of the effort compassion requires, and lead people to step back from it? I test this with choice-based paradigms like the Empathy Selection Task.

Compassion Fatigue

Using daily diary methods, I examine whether disengaging from compassion protects people from compassion fatigue or feeds into it.

Moral Valuation of Compassion

How much people value compassion morally may shape whether they push through its costs, including toward disliked or outgroup targets.

Self-Compassion

An emerging interest: how turning compassion inward relates to mindfulness and to caring for others.
