Ph.D. Student in Social Psychology · Penn State University

I'm a first-year doctoral student working with Dr. Daryl Cameron in the Empathy and Moral Psychology Lab at Penn State. I study why people sometimes choose to avoid compassion, and how practices like mindfulness shape the way we weigh the costs and rewards of caring for others.

Before Penn State, I completed my undergraduate degree at Radford University, where I was a research assistant in the SAP Lab with Dr. Daniel Berry, studying empathy, compassion, intergroup relations, and mindfulness interventions.

Research

Compassion is often treated as an automatic, unlimited response, but it takes effort, and people frequently choose to avoid it. My research examines the motivational costs of caring: when and why people approach or avoid compassion, and what that means for their own well-being over time.

Mindfulness & Compassion Avoidance

Does mindfulness heighten awareness of the effort compassion requires, and lead people to step back from it? I test this with choice-based paradigms like the Empathy Selection Task.

Compassion Fatigue

Using daily diary methods, I examine whether disengaging from compassion protects people from compassion fatigue or feeds into it.

Moral Valuation of Compassion

How much people value compassion morally may shape whether they push through its costs, including toward disliked or outgroup targets.

Self-Compassion

An emerging interest: how turning compassion inward relates to mindfulness and to caring for others.
